About
Song Bio

“Last Christmas” is a Christmas classic by the pop duo Wham!. Despite its upbeat instrumental, it’s a lot more bittersweet and focuses on a breakup as George Michael sings about someone who left him the day after Christmas.

The music video follows the same story, with the added detail that George is brokenhearted after his girlfriend left him specifically for his bandmate, Last Christmas. Through its run, he can’t help but focus on her during a couple’s retreat due to his heartache, so much so that she’s visually set apart from everyone else in the video by her brighter clothes. This unique approach and Wham!’s overall wonderful mucisianship made it a modern holiday superhit and the group’s most successful song.

It hasn’t just been widely covered, but has had multiple covers reach decent notoriety and chart positions, including a version by Ariana Grande which reached #1 on the US Digital Holiday Songs chart and another version by Crazy Frog which reached #16 on UK Singles.

Despite being an overall inoffensive song, “Last Christmas” was actually the subject of some controversy. George Michael was accused of plagiarizing the melody of “Can’t Smile Without You”, which was popularized through covers by both the Carpenters and Barry Manilow. The outcome of the lawsuit that followed is unclear, as some sources say it was settled in exchange for some of George’s royalties, while others say it was dismissed when it was brought up that no less than sixty other songs from the past hundred years also had a similar melody.

What did Wham! say about "Last Christmas"?
Genius Answer

The first time I ever heard it, it was absolutely knockout, it sounded for all the world like a Christmas classic. It’s Christmas distilled if you ask me. It’s the essence of Christmas. And so, its enduring nature, its perennial nature isn’t really a surprise. But it’s a wonderful privilege to have had bestowed upon Wham!, to have one of the all-time Christmas classics — which I think it’s fair to say it probably is now. We’d had a bite to eat and were sitting together relaxing with the television on in the background when, almost unnoticed, George disappeared upstairs for an hour or so, when he came back down, such was his excitement, it was as if he had discovered gold which, in a sense, he had. We went to his old room, the room in which we had spent hours as kids recording pastiches of radio shows and jingles, the room where he kept a keyboard and something on which to record his sparks of inspiration, and he played me the introduction and the beguiling, wistful chorus melody to ‘Last Christmas’. It was a moment of wonder. George had performed musical alchemy, distilling the essence of Christmas into music. Adding a lyric which told the tale of betrayed love was a masterstroke and, as he did so often, he touched hearts.

— via Smooth Radio

How did this song perform on the Billboard charts?
Genius Answer

During the tracking week ending December 14, 2024, the song reached a peak of #3 on the Billboard Hot 100 chart, giving it its highest peak to date at the time. During the tracking week ending December 13, 2025, the song reached it’s highest peak of #2 on the Hot 100, 41 years after its release.
