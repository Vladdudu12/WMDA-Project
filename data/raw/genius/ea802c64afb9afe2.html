About
Song Bio

“Gin and Juice” is Snoop Dogg’s depiction of the more laid back side of the streets. It has arguably one of the catchiest hooks in all of hip-hop and is symbolic of what the G-funk is all about.

It is considered a classic in the mainstream media. It was nominated for “Best Rap Solo Performance” at the 1995 Grammy Awards In 2011, and listed #8 on VH1’s 100 Greatest Hip-Hop Songs.

Instrumental
Genius Answer

Did anyone not get credit for working on this song?
Genius Answer

Former [Tha Dogg Pound] affiliate Emanuel Dean worked extensively on this song. However, he was never credited for his work, which he lamented in an unofficial interview years later:

But I know I did “Gin and Juice”. And I know Dr. Dre cannot play any music. He definitely made the beat, but I mean as far as the music was concerned he watched and I definitely did what I had to do, you know. And I asked him for credit and money, and when he said ‘For what?’, I already had a publishing deal… offered to me by BMI. I let him answer the way he wanted to answer, and when he said ‘For what?’ I was like Okay, cool. Right then and there I was like Okay, I’m going to go with BMI. So the next day I came back to him and I said “Friday’s my last day”. I think I made about five or six thousand dollars working with him. And I did “Gin and Juice”, and I did “Snoop Doggy Dogg” on the second day I knew Dr. Dre. The second day I knew him, I did “Snoop Doggy Dogg”. I didn’t get a platinum record.

Has this track received any certifications?
Genius Answer

“Gin and Juice” was certified gold by the RIAA on April 6, 1994.
